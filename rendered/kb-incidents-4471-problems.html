  • Shared parser reads month first. Every tenant writes the day first. The parser swaps day and month on any date with a day of 12 or less: a booking for 7 April becomes an invite for 4 July. This is the bug #4471 is named for. Ticket.
  • booking-v3 hint says MM/DD. The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, then reads the field day first. People type what the hint says, and the date flips before it reaches the parser. Fixing the parser does not fix this. Ticket.
  • Booking moves one day. Some reports describe a one-day shift, not a swap. The same meeting shows Tuesday in Lisbon and Monday in Toronto, and an invite can arrive before its booking. That is a time-zone conversion bug. The desk noted these reports (oneDayMove) but filed them under the parser. Ticket.

How the reports are placed

A report’s problem comes from where it was made, not from its subject line or its intake tag:

  1. Take each report once. The export repeats some reports across pages, so count by id.
  2. Tenant and channel decide the cause (causes.ts): a day-first tenant using the booking-v3 form has the hint problem. Every other report from a day-first tenant has the parser problem.
  3. A report the desk read as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) has the time-zone problem, whatever its tenant.

The intake tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser. It disagrees with the tenant on dozens of these reports, in both directions, so it does not decide the problem.
